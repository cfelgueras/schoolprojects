## Paper 1 or Paper 2?

| Type of question | Paper |
|---|---|
| Gradient, midpoint, distance with exact values | **Paper 1** — by hand |
| Equation of a line, parallel / perpendicular, perpendicular bisector | **Paper 1** — by hand |
| Converse of Pythagoras (is it right-angled?) | **Paper 1** — by hand |
| Intersection of two lines with awkward numbers | **Paper 2** — EQUA SIML or GRAPH ISCT |
| Distance / lengths with decimals in a context question | **Paper 2** — RUN-MAT |

Straight lines are mostly a Paper 1 topic, so practise doing them **by hand**. On Paper 2 use the GDC to save time and to check.

## What you need to be able to do

- [ ] Calculate a distance with $\sqrt{\ }$ in **RUN-MAT** and keep exact fractions with the fraction key.
- [ ] Solve two linear equations simultaneously in **EQUA → SIML**.
- [ ] Draw two lines in **GRAPH** and find where they meet with **G-Solv → ISCT**.

## Step by step

### A. Distance between two points

**Example:** distance from $(1,\ 1)$ to $(8,\ 2)$.

1. [[MENU]] → **RUN-MAT**.
2. Type [[SHIFT]] [[x²]] (√) then `(8−1)` [[x²]] `+` `(2−1)` [[x²]] and [[EXE]].
3. Answer: $\sqrt{50} \approx 7.07$.

### B. Intersection of two lines with EQUA

**Example:** $3x + 2y = 7$ and $5x - 4y = 2$.

1. [[MENU]] → **EQUA** → [[F1]] **SIML**.
2. Number of unknowns: [[F1]] **2**.
3. The screen shows a table for $a_n x + b_n y = c_n$. Enter row 1: `3` [[EXE]] `2` [[EXE]] `7` [[EXE]]; row 2: `5` [[EXE]] `(-)4` [[EXE]] `2` [[EXE]].
4. [[F1]] **SOLV**: $x = \dfrac{16}{11} \approx 1.45$, $y = \dfrac{29}{22} \approx 1.32$.

Write the equations in the form $ax + by = c$ **before** you enter them.

### C. Intersection of two lines with GRAPH

1. [[MENU]] → **GRAPH**. Rearrange each line to $y = \ldots$ and enter them in Y1 and Y2.
2. [[F6]] DRAW → [[SHIFT]] [[F5]] **G-SOLV** → [[F5]] **ISCT**.
3. Read the point. If the lines are off screen, change the window with [[SHIFT]] [[F3]] V-WIN.
